# Laycal — product brief for the demo pitch deck

This brief is for whoever designs the demo deck. It says what Laycal is, who it is for and how to talk about it. Read "Positioning" and "Do and don't" first; they matter more than the feature list.

## One line

**Laycal calls every new lead within a minute, qualifies them and books the meeting on your calendar.**

Short version for a title slide: *Be the first call every lead gets.*

## Positioning

- **Category:** speed to lead. An AI voice agent that phones a new lead the moment they ask to be contacted.
- **Buyer:** the owner or the person who runs sales or marketing at a business where the first company to call usually wins the job.
- **Promise:** no lead waits. Every form submission gets a phone call in seconds, day or night, and interested leads end up as booked meetings.
- **What Laycal is not:** a cold-calling dialer, a tool for SDR teams to blast lists, or a cheaper alternative to another product. Do not frame it any of these ways.

## Who it is for

Any business where a fast first call decides who gets the customer. Three examples to show in the deck:

| Segment | The lead | What the call does |
|---|---|---|
| Home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical) | Someone requests a quote or a repair | Confirms the job and the address, books the visit |
| E-commerce | Someone asks about a high-value product or abandons a quote | Answers questions, books a consultation or callback |
| B2B | Someone fills in a demo or contact form | Qualifies on role, team size and timeline, books the meeting |

The product works the same way for all three. The business sets the questions to ask, the objections to expect and what gets booked.

## The problem

1. A lead fills in a form while they are interested. They often contact several companies at once.
2. Most businesses call back hours later, or the next day, because staff are busy, it is after hours, or nobody is watching the inbox.
3. By then the lead has spoken to someone else. The business paid for a lead it never reached.

Keep this in the buyer's own terms: missed calls, slow follow-up, leads paid for and lost.

## How it works

Three steps. This is the core slide.

1. **Connect your lead source.** A website form, Google Form, ad lead form or CRM sends each new lead to Laycal.
2. **Laycal calls within seconds.** The AI agent introduces itself as calling from the business, asks the qualifying questions and handles objections.
3. **The meeting lands on your calendar.** The agent offers real open slots from Google Calendar and creates the event while the lead is still on the phone.

After each call the business gets the outcome, a recording and a written summary.

## What the product includes

Use these as supporting points, not as the headline.

- **An instant call on every lead.** Each submission triggers its own call. There is no queue.
- **A guided assistant builder.** The business answers a few questions about its trade, hours and services. Laycal writes the agent's script. No prompt writing.
- **Calendar booking during the call.** Google Calendar is built in. The agent only offers times that are free.
- **Every call recorded and summarised.** Outcome, recording, transcript summary and the details captured (name, email, appointment time).
- **A live dashboard.** Calls placed, answer rate, meetings booked, cost.
- **Your own phone numbers.** Works with Twilio, Plivo and Vonage accounts.
- **Voice choice.** Several male and female voices; English, Spanish, French and German.
- **Handles bursts.** Many calls can run at the same time, so a busy hour does not create a backlog.

## Second use: lead reactivation

Laycal can also call a list the business already has. Upload a CSV of old leads, the agent calls each one, and the interested ones get booked.

Line for this: *Turn the leads you already paid for into booked meetings.*

If this appears in the deck, give it one slide after the main story. Say that lists must be opted-in, and that consent and do-not-call compliance are the customer's responsibility.

## Pricing and access

- **Pay as you go.** The business pays per calling minute. No seats, no plans, no monthly minimum.
- **The per-minute rate is agreed per account** and confirmed on the demo. Leave it as a blank to fill in per prospect: `$__ per minute`.
- **Assistants:** the first one is free. Each additional assistant is a one-time $10.
- **Credits:** the business tops up a balance and calls draw from it.
- **Invite-only.** There is no public sign-up. After the demo, a fit gets an email invitation that creates the account.

Line for the pricing slide: *Pay for the calls. Nothing else.*

## Do and don't

**Do**

- Lead with speed: "called within a minute", "within seconds", "the first call".
- Talk about outcomes the buyer cares about: leads reached, meetings booked.
- Use plain business language. The audience is an HVAC owner as much as a sales director.
- Offer to play a real call recording. That is the proof.

**Don't**

- Don't quote response-time or conversion statistics. We have none of our own to cite. Use the prospect's own numbers.
- Don't name or compare against competitors.
- Don't describe it as cheap, affordable or a budget option.
- Don't lead with technology (AI models, Vapi, webhooks, multi-tenancy). Mention how it connects only where it answers "will this work with my form?".
- Don't claim customer results, logos or testimonials. There are none to show yet.
- Don't promise integrations beyond those listed above.

## Suggested deck flow

1. **Title** — Be the first call every lead gets.
2. **The problem** — leads go cold while they wait for a call back.
3. **What it costs** — the prospect's own numbers: leads per month, how many they reach today, what a booked meeting is worth.
4. **The answer** — Laycal calls every new lead within a minute.
5. **How it works** — the three steps.
6. **Hear it** — play a real call recording.
7. **What you see afterwards** — dashboard, recording, summary, the calendar event.
8. **Who it is for** — home services, e-commerce, B2B.
9. **Setup** — connect a form, build the assistant, connect the calendar.
10. **Pricing** — pay per minute, first assistant free.
11. **Next step** — get an invitation.

## Look and feel

Match the website (laycal.com).

- **Dark, black and white.** Near-black backgrounds, white and grey text.
- **One accent colour:** blue `#2D6AFF`. Use it sparingly, for the key number or the call to action.
- **No multi-colour gradients.** Green, amber and red appear only as small status dots.
- **Type:** Geist for text, Geist Mono for small labels and numbers.
- **Imagery:** real product screens (lead table, live call, recording player, booked meeting, dashboard) rather than stock photos or illustrations of robots.
- **Logo:** `public/assets/logo.png` (symbol only), next to the word "Laycal".
- **Tone:** confident, direct, short sentences. One idea per slide.

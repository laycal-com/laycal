Following up with leads feels like chasing when it is improvised: a call when someone remembers, a second one a week later, a message that says "just checking in". It feels like service when it is planned: a quick first call, a clear message if they miss it, and a sensible end point.

This guide gives you a sequence you can use as it is, what to say at each step, and how to know when to stop.

## Follow-up starts with the first call

The best follow-up is a first call that happens while the lead still remembers asking. If you call within minutes, many leads answer and the follow-up never needs to start. If you call the next day, you are following up on a lead that has already cooled. So before refining the sequence, make sure the first step is fast; [lead response](/blogs/lead-response) covers how.

## A simple sequence for new leads

This works for most businesses where a lead fills in a form and expects a call: home services, B2B demos, consultations, quotes.

1. **Within a minute: call.** Use the details from the form so the lead knows why you are calling.
2. **If there is no answer: send a short text or email straight away.** Say who you are, what they asked about and that you will try again. Keep it to two lines.
3. **Later the same day: call again,** at a different time of day from the first attempt.
4. **The next day: one more call,** again at a different time. Morning if you tried in the afternoon, early evening if you tried at lunchtime.
5. **Then stop calling.** Send one final message saying you will not keep calling, and how they can reach you or book a time themselves.

Three calls over about a day is a reasonable default. Some businesses add a fourth attempt for high-value leads. Fewer than two tells you who answered first time, not who was interested.

## What to say when they answer

Open with the request, not with your company history. (The examples below use a made-up roofing company.)

> "Hi Sarah, this is Tom from Brightline Roofing. You asked about a leak on our website a few minutes ago. Have you got a minute to tell me what's going on?"

Then work through the questions that tell you whether the lead is a fit and what they need. Keep it a conversation; [what to ask a new lead on the first call](/blogs/what-to-ask-a-new-lead-on-the-first-call) has the questions by type of business. End with a concrete next step: a booked appointment, a site visit, a demo. "We'll be in touch" is not a next step.

## What to say when they do not answer

### The voicemail

Short, specific and with a reason to pick up next time.

> "Hi Sarah, Tom from Brightline Roofing, calling about the leak you mentioned on our website. I'll try you again this afternoon, or you can call me back on this number."

### The message after a missed call

> "Hi Sarah, it's Tom from Brightline Roofing. I just tried you about the leak you reported. I'll call again around 4pm, or reply here with a better time."

Naming the next call time makes it far more likely they will answer it.

### The last message

> "Hi Sarah, I've tried a few times about your roof and don't want to keep calling. If you'd still like someone to take a look, reply here or book a time at the link below."

This closes the loop politely, and it often gets a reply from people who were simply busy.

## Mistakes that make follow-up feel like chasing

- **No context.** "Just checking in" makes the lead do the work of remembering who you are.
- **Calling at the same time every day.** If they were in a meeting at 10am on Monday, they may well be at 10am on Tuesday too.
- **Long gaps between attempts.** A week between calls means each call is effectively a first call to someone who has forgotten.
- **No end point.** Calling indefinitely annoys people and wastes your time. Decide the number of attempts in advance.
- **Calling outside reasonable hours.** Fast is good; 7am on a Sunday is not. Set a calling window and check the rules where you operate.

## Follow-up after a quote is a different job

Everything above is about a new lead who asked to be contacted. Following up after you have sent a quote or an estimate is a later stage: the person knows you, has your price and is deciding. The tone and timing are different, and it deserves its own plan.

## Keep a record of every attempt

Write down each call, each message and each outcome. It stops two people calling the same lead, it shows you which times of day people actually answer, and it lets you see how many leads were reached in the end versus how many were simply never called back.

Your website takes enquiries at any hour. Your team does not. The leads that arrive in between, in the evening, overnight and at the weekend, are the ones most likely to wait a long time for a call, and the ones least likely to be noticed waiting.

This guide covers how to find out how many of your leads that is, what typically happens to them, and what you can do about it.

## Check how many of yours arrive out of hours

Do not assume. Count.

1. Export the last 30 days of form submissions with their date and time.
2. Mark each one as inside or outside your working hours. Remember weekends and public holidays.
3. Work out the share that arrived outside.

Many businesses are surprised by the result, in both directions. People often fill in forms when they have a quiet moment, which for a homeowner or a busy manager may well be after work. But your own pattern is the only one that matters, and it takes ten minutes to see.

## What usually happens to them

The form sends a notification to an inbox. Nobody is watching the inbox. The lead sits there until the next working morning.

By then a few things have changed:

- **The lead has moved on with their evening.** The need that made them fill in the form at 8pm is no longer front of mind at 10am.
- **They may have asked someone else.** If another business got back to them first, your call arrives as the second option.
- **The morning has its own queue.** Overnight leads compete with everything else that needs doing first thing, and some slip to the afternoon or the next day.

None of this shows up as a problem unless you look for it. The lead simply does not answer, and it gets logged as a lead that went cold.

## Your options

There is no single right answer. Here are the realistic choices, with what each one does and does not solve.

### An automatic reply

An email or text that goes out as soon as the form is submitted: "Thanks, we'll call you tomorrow morning."

It is easy to set up and it sets an expectation, which is better than silence. It does not start a conversation, and it does not stop the lead from continuing to look.

### An on-call rota

Someone on the team takes new leads in the evening and at weekends, with notifications going to their phone.

This works when lead volume out of hours is low and the leads are valuable. It costs people their evenings, so it tends to be followed closely at first and loosely after a few months.

### An outsourced call team

A service makes the callbacks for you outside your hours.

You get a person on the phone. They work from a script, they usually cannot see your calendar, and the result is often a message for you to follow up the next day, which leaves the booking still to do.

### An AI voice agent

An agent phones the lead as soon as the form is submitted, asks your qualifying questions and books the meeting into your calendar on the call.

This is what Laycal does. The lead gets a call while they are still thinking about the request, and your team starts the morning with booked meetings instead of a list to work through. You should listen to real calls before deciding, because how it sounds matters; we play recordings on the demo.

## Decide when you are willing to call

Responding fast is not the same as calling at any hour. Someone who submits a form at 8pm will usually welcome a call a minute later. Someone who submits at 2am probably will not.

Set a calling window that suits your customers, and check the rules on calling hours where you operate, since many places restrict when businesses may phone people. A lead that arrives outside the window should be first in line when it opens.

## Start with the count

Before you change anything, do the ten-minute count in the first section. If very few of your leads arrive out of hours, the fix is elsewhere, and [measuring your overall response time](/blogs/how-to-measure-lead-response-time) will show you where.

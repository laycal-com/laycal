The first call to a new lead is short, and it is easy to waste. Some callers treat it as a sales pitch and lose the lead in the first minute. Others chat pleasantly and hang up without a next step.

The call has three jobs: confirm what the lead asked for, find out whether you can help, and book what happens next. Everything you ask should serve one of those.

## Open by saying why you are calling

The lead filled in a form a moment ago, or a day ago. Either way, start by connecting the call to that request.

> "Hi, this is Sam from Northside Heating. You asked us for a quote on a boiler replacement, so I'm calling to get a few details and find a time for a visit. Is now all right?"

That opening tells them who you are, why you are calling and how long it will take. It also asks permission, which makes the rest of the call easier.

## The five questions

You will not always need all five, and the wording changes by business. The order matters: start with what they want and end with logistics.

1. **What do you need?** Ask them to describe it in their own words, even if the form already says. "Tell me a bit more about what's going on" gets you detail the form never will.
2. **How soon?** Urgency decides everything that follows. Someone with no heating in winter and someone planning for next spring need different next steps.
3. **Who else is involved in the decision?** Ask it plainly: "Is it just you deciding, or is someone else involved?" It saves you booking a meeting with the wrong person.
4. **Is there anything that would rule this out?** Budget is the obvious one, but so are location, timing and technical constraints. Ask it as a practical question, not a challenge.
5. **What do I need to know to book this?** The address, access, the best number, the people who should be on the call.

## How the questions change by business

**Home services** (heating, plumbing, roofing, electrical)

- What is the problem, and when did it start?
- Is it urgent, or can it wait a few days?
- What is the address, and is it a house or a flat?
- Will someone be home, and is there anything we should know about access?

**E-commerce** (considered or made-to-order products)

- What are you looking for, and what will it be used for?
- Do you have sizes, quantities or a specification in mind?
- When do you need it by?
- Would a short consultation help, or do you have what you need to decide?

**B2B** (software, agencies, professional services)

- What prompted you to get in touch now?
- How are you handling this today?
- How many people would use it, and what is your role in the decision?
- What timeline are you working to?

## What not to ask

- **Anything they already typed into the form.** Confirm it; do not make them repeat it. "I have you at 14 Elm Road, is that right?" is better than "What's your address?"
- **A full discovery interview.** The first call earns the second conversation. Save the deep questions for the meeting.
- **Leading questions.** "You'd agree that speed matters, wouldn't you?" tells the lead they are being handled.

## When they push back

Three responses come up on almost every first call.

- **"I'm just looking."** Take it at face value. "That's fine. What would be useful to know at this stage?" keeps the conversation going without pressure.
- **"Can you just send me some information?"** Agree, and ask one question so you send the right thing: "Of course. What matters most to you, so I send what's relevant?"
- **"How much is it?"** Give a straight answer if you can. If the price depends on details, say what it depends on and offer the quickest way to get a real figure.

## End with a specific next step

Do not finish with "we'll be in touch". Offer two concrete times and let them pick.

> "I can have someone with you Thursday at 10 or Friday at 2. Which suits you better?"

Then confirm the details back: the time, the address or the meeting link, and their email address, spelled out. Tell them what will happen next and who they will hear from.

## Writing this down once

The questions above are a script, and a script only helps if it is used on every call. Write yours down: the opening line, the questions in order, your answers to the objections you hear most, and what gets booked at the end.

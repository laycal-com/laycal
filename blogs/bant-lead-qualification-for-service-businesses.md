BANT is a lead qualification framework that salespeople have been taught for decades. It stands for four things you want to know before you spend time on a lead:

- **Budget:** can they pay for what you sell?
- **Authority:** are they the person who decides?
- **Need:** do they have a problem you solve?
- **Timeline:** when do they want it solved?

It was built for big sales teams selling to companies. Used as written, it sounds like an interrogation to a homeowner with a leaking roof. Used with some care, it is a good checklist for the first call to any new lead.

## Why the usual order is wrong for service businesses

BANT is usually taught in its own order: budget first. On a first call to someone who just filled in your form, that is backwards. They asked you for help a few minutes ago. If your first question is about money, you sound like you are deciding whether they are worth your time.

Ask in the order the lead thinks about it instead:

1. **Need:** what is going on?
2. **Timeline:** how soon does it need fixing?
3. **Authority:** who else is involved?
4. **Budget:** only what you need to book the right visit.

Some people call this order NTAB. The four questions are the same; the conversation is easier.

## Need: let them describe it

The form told you something, but rarely enough. Ask an open question and let them talk.

> "You mentioned a problem with the air conditioning. Can you tell me what it's doing?"

Then ask the two or three follow-ups that change what you send or book. For a roofer, that is whether water is coming in now, where it shows up and whether there was a storm. For an HVAC company, whether it is the heating or the cooling, and whether the system runs at all. Our pages for [roofing companies](/roofing) and [HVAC companies](/hvac) list the questions we start from for each trade.

For a B2B service, need sounds different but works the same way: "What made you get in touch this week?" tells you more than any form field.

## Timeline: urgency decides the next step

Timeline is the question that changes the most. Someone with no heating in January needs a visit tomorrow. Someone planning a new roof before next winter needs an estimate, not an emergency slot.

> "Is this something you need sorted this week, or are you planning ahead?"

If the answer is "this week", book now, on this call. If it is "in a few months", book the estimate anyway, or agree when you will call back. A lead with a long timeline is still a lead.

## Authority: ask it plainly

In home services, authority usually means: is there a partner, a landlord or a property manager who has to agree? In B2B, it means: who else signs off?

> "Is it just you deciding, or should anyone else be there for the visit?"

This saves you a wasted trip, or a meeting with someone who cannot say yes. Ask it once, without suspicion, and believe the answer.

## Budget: ask less than you think

Most service businesses cannot quote on the first call, and most leads know it. You do not need their budget. You need to know whether anything rules the job out, and they need to know what the first step costs.

So answer the budget question rather than asking it:

> "The inspection is a set fee, and you get a written estimate before any work starts."

If your service has a real minimum (a replacement that starts at a certain price, a retainer, a minimum order), say it now. It is kinder than letting someone book a visit they cannot afford. For B2B, a range works: "Projects like this usually run between X and Y. Does that fit what you had in mind?"

## What to do with the answers

After four questions you have one of three outcomes:

- **A fit.** Book the next step on the same call. Offer two specific times, confirm the address and the contact details, and tell them who is coming.
- **Not yet.** The need is real but the timeline is long, or someone else has to agree. Agree a date to call again and write it down.
- **Not a fit.** Wrong area, a job you do not do, or a budget far below your minimum. Say so politely, and point them somewhere useful if you can.

Every lead should end the call in one of those three, never in "we'll be in touch".
